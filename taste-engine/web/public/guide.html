The rules

Vote on aesthetic quality, not personal preference.

Don't vote based on

  • Brand recognition
  • Whether you like the product
  • Familiarity or trends
  • Personal taste in aesthetics
  • Whether you'd use the site

Do vote based on

  • Layout and spacing
  • Typography and hierarchy
  • Color discipline
  • Grid structure and composition
  • Motion and interaction quality
  • Overall cohesion and craft

If you can't decide: vote for the one you'd rather show a client. If truly torn, press Space to skip — don't force it.

What to keep in mind

When comparing two sites, consider these dimensions. You don't need to think about all of them — just the ones that stand out in this specific comparison.

W

Whitespace

Is the negative space intentional? Does it create breathing room or feel empty? Is the density deliberate?

T

Typography

Is the type hierarchy clear? Is the scale, weight, and spacing deliberate? Does the type feel like an afterthought or a design element?

C

Color

Is the palette cohesive? Does it support the brand or fight it? Is there discipline or chaos?

G

Grid & Layout

Is the structure disciplined? Does it break the grid with purpose or by accident? Do elements align?

M

Motion

(if visible) Does the animation feel smooth and intentional? Does it enhance or distract?

X

Texture & Imagery

Are the images high quality? Do they feel art-directed or like stock photos? Is there grain, texture, flatness?

O

Overall cohesion

Does everything feel like it belongs together? Is there a unified aesthetic statement?

Key insight: a good design is not one that maximizes all dimensions. It's one that achieves coherent tension — deliberate trade-offs that create a unified statement. A brutalist site with zero whitespace can be elite. An airy editorial site with minimal type can be elite. What matters is whether the choices feel intentional.

How to write reasoning

Your reasoning becomes the training label. Vague reasoning teaches the model nothing. Specific reasoning teaches it exactly what to look for.

Useless (vague)

  • "A looks cleaner"
  • "B is boring"
  • "A is more premium"
  • "I just like A"
  • "B feels cluttered"
  • "A has better vibes"

These provide no dimensional data — the model can't learn from them.

Valuable (specific)

  • "A's whitespace is more generous — the content has more breathing room"
  • "B's typography is flat — no hierarchy between heading and body"
  • "A's color palette is monochromatic with one accent — B uses four competing colors"
  • "A's grid is rigorous — elements align to a clear structure"
  • "B has too many competing elements — badges and CTAs fight for attention"
  • "A's type has more contrast — large display type against small body text"

These isolate specific features — the model knows exactly what to learn.

The formula

[Winner] is better because [specific feature] — [what it does for the design].

Good

"A is better because the whitespace is more generous — it gives the content room to breathe."

Good

"B is better because the type hierarchy is clearer — display type is dramatically larger than body."

Good

FAQ

What if the sites look identical?

Look closer. There's always a difference — whitespace, type scale, color temperature, image quality. If you genuinely can't find one, press Space to skip.

Does my reasoning have to be long?

No. 1–2 sentences is perfect. Just be specific.

Do I need to enter my name every time?

No — it's saved on your device after the first visit.

What if I clicked A but changed my mind?

In the reasoning box, hit ← Back to return to the pair and pick differently.

What happens to my votes?

They're stored in a database and used to train the AI model. Your reasoning becomes the training label.

Can I see how I'm doing?

Yes — visit the Leaderboard page to see your agreement rate with other voters.

What if I don't know design terminology?

That's fine. Describe what you see in your own words. "The text is bigger and bolder" is just as useful as "the type hierarchy has more contrast."

Should I vote on mobile or desktop?

Desktop. The sites are rendered at 1440×900.
