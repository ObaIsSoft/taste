Voter guide

Thank you for helping. Your votes teach a model what well-made web design looks like, so it can help designers and steer AI tools away from generic results. Please read this once before your first session.

Before you start

  • Your invite link signs you in. Keep it private: it is your key. If you were given a code instead, type it on the first screen.
  • A computer is best. It shows both sites at once. On a phone you see one at a time: switch between them with the A and B tabs, and look at both before you vote.
  • Short sessions. 20 to 30 votes at a time work best; stop when your attention drops. Every vote is saved straight away, and a refresh shows the same pair again.
  • There are no right answers. We want your judgment, not a guess at anyone else's.

How the rounds work

There are two rounds. Choose one on the first screen; you can switch at any time.

Visual round
The screens of two sites, A and B, from the top of each page down. Scroll through each before you decide. It is about how the sites look.
Motion round
A short recording of each site, made the same way for every site: the page loading and its intro, a slow scroll down and back, the pointer resting on a few links, and the menu opened. Recordings are silent. Play both, and judge only the motion: how things move, not how they look standing still.

Each round asks for a set number of votes in the visual round and a set number of in the motion round; the progress bar counts them, and you can keep going afterwards if you like. A motion vote takes longer, because you watch two recordings.

Calibration, and pairs that come back

  • The first pairs are the same for everyone (a set of in the visual round, a set of in the motion round), in your own order. Comparing everyone's answers on the same pairs shows how far designers agree, which is the most important thing the pilot measures. The progress bar says "Calibration" until you have done them.
  • About a few pairs come back later, with the sides swapped. Answer them fresh, as if new: do not try to remember what you said. Changing your mind is fine; it tells us how close the call was.
  • After that, pairs are drawn so that every site is compared about as often as every other.

Making a call

  1. Choose A is better, B is better, Equally good or Can't decide.
  2. Say what decided it: tap any of the listed things, or write it in your own words, as a term of your own or a whole sentence. For A or B, at least one is needed; for Equally good and Can't decide it is optional, and welcome.
  3. Add a sentence about why if you like. Now and then the page asks for one; not every vote does.
  4. Submit. The next pair appears.
Equally good
Both are well made and you have no preference.
Can't decide
You cannot judge this pair, for example two strengths you cannot weigh against each other. It does not count as a tie.
Report
At the top of each site: use it when the capture is broken: blank, an error, a cookie wall or pop-up covering it, or the wrong site. That capture is taken out for checking.
Open live site
Optional, for a closer look. Judge the screens or the recording you were shown, not the live site, which may have changed.

Judge craft, not preference

  • Ignore the brand, the product, and whether you would use it.
  • Ignore whether the style is in fashion. Judge how well it is done.
  • Ignore the language a site is written in.
  • Tie-breaker: which one would you rather show a client?

Great design is not the most of everything. Look for deliberate trade-offs that add up to one clear statement: a dense brutalist page and an airy minimal one can both be excellent.

Writing a reason

Name a specific feature and what it does for the design. For example:

A is better because one large headline sits over quiet body text, which gives the page a clear starting point.

Avoid feelings without evidence, such as "looks cleaner" or "better vibe". Only mention motion you actually saw.

Words used here

Pair
Two sites, A and B, shown together for one vote.
Round
Visual (screens) or motion (recordings).
Calibration
The first pairs, the same for every voter.
Repeat
A pair shown again later with the sides swapped.

Your own words
A term or phrase of your own for what decided it, kept exactly as you wrote it.
Reason
A sentence about why.

On a computer

1 A is better, 2 B is better, E equally good, S can't decide. Then the number keys pick what decided it, ↵ submits and Esc goes back.

Your data

With your voter name we store your votes, what you tapped and wrote, how long each vote took, and how it was seen: the size of your screen, whether you used a phone, which site you opened on a phone, whether you scrolled and played each site, and when you opened a live site. This is how we tell taste apart from habits such as always favouring one side, and it is never shown to other voters. Names are never shown publicly.
