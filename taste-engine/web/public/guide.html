Voter guide

Thank you for helping. Your votes teach a model what well-made web design looks like, so it can help designers and steer AI tools away from generic results.

How a session works

  1. Open your invite link. It signs you in, so keep it private. If you were given a code instead, type it on the first screen.
  2. Pick a round. The visual round shows the screens of two sites, A and B: scroll through each. The motion round shows a short recording of each: play both.
  3. Choose A is better, B is better, Equally good or Can't decide.
  4. If you chose A or B, tap the one to three things that decided it, such as typography or pacing. When asked, write one sentence about why. Not every vote asks.

Sessions of 20 to 30 votes work best. Every vote is saved straight away, and a refresh shows the same pair again. On a phone, switch between the sites with the A and B tabs.

Judge craft, not preference

  • Ignore the brand, the product, and whether you would use it.
  • Ignore whether the style is in fashion. Judge how well it is done.
  • Tie-breaker: which one would you rather show a client?

Great design is not the most of everything. Look for deliberate trade-offs that add up to one clear statement: a dense brutalist page and an airy minimal one can both be excellent.

What to look at

Whitespace
Is the empty space doing work, or just empty?
Typography
Is the hierarchy clear? Are scale, weight and spacing deliberate?
Colour
Is the palette disciplined and does it support the content?
Grid and layout
Is the structure consistent, and are breaks from it purposeful?
Texture and imagery
Is the imagery art-directed, or generic stock?
Overall cohesion
Do the choices add up to one statement?
Motion round
Smoothness, pacing, how long the intro makes you wait, how scrolling feels, hover and click feedback, and the motion overall.

Writing a reason

Name a specific feature and what it does for the design. For example:

A is better because one large headline sits over quiet body text, which gives the page a clear starting point.

Avoid feelings without evidence, such as "looks cleaner" or "better vibe". Only mention motion you actually saw.

Equally good, can't decide, or broken

  • Equally good: both are well made and you have no preference.
  • Can't decide: you cannot judge this pair. It does not count as a tie.
  • Report, at the top of a site: it is blank, shows an error, a cookie wall or the wrong site. That capture is taken out for checking.

On a computer

1 A is better, 2 B is better, E equally good, S can't decide. Then 1 to 6 pick what decided it, ↵ submits and Esc goes back.
