# TASTE Engine — Voter Guide

**Welcome.** You're helping build the first AI system that can judge design quality. Your votes and reasoning will train a model to distinguish elite design from generic design. This guide explains how to vote, what to look for, and how to write reasoning that teaches the model something real.

---

## How voting works

1. **Enter your name** (first visit only — it's saved on your device)
2. You'll see **two websites side by side** — Site A (left) and Site B (right)
3. Look at both carefully — scroll, hover, explore
4. Click the one that's **better designed** (A or B)
5. A box pops up asking **why** — type 1–2 sentences citing specific features
6. Hit **Submit** (or Enter) — next pair loads

That's it. Each vote takes 30–60 seconds.

**Keyboard shortcuts:** `A` = pick left · `B` = pick right · `Space` = skip/draw · `F` = toggle hero/full page view

## The rules

**Vote on aesthetic quality, not personal preference.**

| ❌ Don't vote based on... | ✅ Do vote based on... |
|---|---|
| Brand recognition | Layout and spacing |
| Whether you like the product | Typography and hierarchy |
| Familiarity or trends | Color discipline |
| Personal taste in aesthetics | Grid structure and composition |
| Whether you'd use the site | Motion and interaction quality |
| | Overall cohesion and craft |

**There are no wrong answers.** Your taste is the data. We're not testing whether you have "good" taste — we're collecting your taste so the model can learn it.

**If you can't decide:** vote for the one you'd rather show a client. If truly torn, skip the pair — don't force it.

## What to keep in mind

When comparing two sites, consider these dimensions. You don't need to think about all of them — just the ones that stand out in this specific comparison.

| Dimension | Questions to ask yourself |
|---|---|
| **Whitespace** | Is the negative space intentional? Does it create breathing room or feel empty? Is the density deliberate? |
| **Typography** | Is the type hierarchy clear? Is the scale, weight, and spacing deliberate? Does the type feel like an afterthought or a design element? |
| **Color** | Is the palette cohesive? Does it support the brand or fight it? Is there discipline or chaos? |
| **Grid & Layout** | Is the structure disciplined? Does it break the grid with purpose or by accident? Do elements align? |
| **Motion** | (if visible) Does the animation feel smooth and intentional? Does it enhance or distract? |
| **Texture & Imagery** | Are the images high quality? Do they feel art-directed or like stock photos? Is there grain, texture, flatness? |
| **Overall cohesion** | Does everything feel like it belongs together? Is there a unified aesthetic statement? |

**The key insight:** a good design is not one that maximizes all dimensions. It's one that achieves **coherent tension** — deliberate trade-offs that create a unified statement. A brutalist site with zero whitespace can be elite. An airy editorial site with minimal type can be elite. What matters is whether the choices feel intentional.

## How to write reasoning

**This is the most important part.** Your reasoning becomes the training label. Vague reasoning teaches the model nothing. Specific reasoning teaches it exactly what to look for.

### Be specific — cite features, not feelings

| ❌ Useless | ✅ Valuable |
|---|---|
| "A looks cleaner" | "A's whitespace ratio is higher — the content has more breathing room" |
| "B is boring" | "B's typography is flat — no hierarchy between heading and body text" |
| "A is more premium" | "A's color palette is monochromatic with one accent — B uses four competing colors" |
| "I just like A" | "A's grid is rigorous — elements align to a clear structure. B's layout feels arbitrary" |
| "B feels cluttered" | "B has too many competing elements — badges, banners, and CTAs all fight for attention" |
| "A has better vibes" | "A's typography has more contrast — large display type against small body text. B is all one size" |

### The formula

```
"[Winner] is better because [specific feature] — [what it does for the design]."
```

Examples:
- "A is better because the whitespace is more generous — it gives the content room to breathe and makes the typography feel intentional."
- "B is better because the type hierarchy is clearer — the display type is dramatically larger than the body, creating a clear reading order."
- "A is better because the color palette is more disciplined — monochromatic with one accent color, while B uses four competing colors."

### What makes reasoning good

- **Cites a specific feature** (whitespace, typography, color, grid, motion, texture)
- **Explains the effect** (what that feature does for the design)
- **Compares** (how the two sites differ on that feature)
- **Is 1–2 sentences** (you don't need an essay)

### What makes reasoning bad

- **Only expresses a feeling** ("looks better," "feels premium")
- **Doesn't compare** (just describes one site)
- **Is too vague** ("clean," "modern," "professional")
- **Cites something you can't see** (don't claim "GSAP animations" unless you actually saw them)

## FAQ

**Q: What if the sites look identical?**
A: Look closer. There's always a difference — whitespace, type scale, color temperature, image quality. If you genuinely can't find one, skip the pair.

**Q: Does my reasoning have to be long?**
A: No. 1–2 sentences is perfect. Just be specific.

**Q: Can I vote on the same pair twice?**
A: No — each pair is shown once. If you see a duplicate, refresh the page.

**Q: What happens to my votes?**
A: They're stored in a database and used to train the AI model. Your reasoning becomes the training label — it's what the model learns to say when it critiques a design.

**Q: Can I see how I'm doing?**
A: Yes — your agreement with other voters is tracked. High agreement means your taste is consistent with the group. Low agreement might mean you're seeing something others aren't (which is also valuable).

**Q: What if I don't know design terminology?**
A: That's fine. Describe what you see in your own words. "The text is bigger and bolder" is just as useful as "the type hierarchy has more contrast."

**Q: Should I vote on mobile or desktop?**
A: Desktop. The sites are rendered at 1440×900.

**Q: Do I need to enter my name every time?**
A: No — it's saved on your device after the first visit. If you want to vote under a different name, clear your browser's localStorage for this site.

**Q: What if I clicked A but changed my mind?**
A: In the reasoning box, hit **← Back** to return to the pair and pick differently.
